01

Approach

I sit between the bench and the terminal.

I'm doing my Ph.D. at the Universidad de La Laguna, in a group that screens small molecules against human tumour cell lines. I run the experiments, and I work like a data scientist or an engineer on everything around them: the counting, the curve fitting, the image analysis, the sequencing.

My job is to find the step that is slow, subjective or impossible to scale, and replace it with something reproducible.

  1. Find the bottleneckWhat takes hours, or changes depending on who does it?
  2. Get the data outImages, plate readings and sequencing reads, in a form you can work with.
  3. Model itStatistics, computer vision or machine learning, whichever is simplest.
  4. Ship a toolA script, a pipeline or a small app that others in the lab can run.
  5. Back to the benchCheck it against the biology, then use it on the next experiment.

Every lab, and every team, has a step that is still done by hand, the same way, every week. That is usually where I start.

About the figures Every figure and demo on this page is simulated in your browser. They show the kind of problem and the kind of solution, not unpublished data, code or results.

Problems solved

Four problems from the lab

Problem 01 · computer vision

Counting colonies by eye doesn't scale

The problem
Colony assays end with someone counting dots on dozens of plates. It is slow, tiring, and two people rarely get the same number.
What I built
An image pipeline that finds and counts colonies from a photo of the plate, and outputs the stats per condition.
Try it
Click the colonies yourself, then let the algorithm count. Compare.

Python · OpenCV-style segmentation · statistics

Outside biologyObject detection and counting, visual quality control.

Problem 02 · data pipeline

A plate full of numbers, one decision

The problem
A screening plate is 96 absorbance values in a spreadsheet. Someone has to turn them into a potency for every compound and decide which ones go forward.
What I built
A pipeline from raw plate reads to fitted dose-response curves and a ranking by GI50, the dose that halves growth.
Try it
Click a row of the plate to see that compound's curve. The ranking updates itself.

Python / R · four-parameter logistic fits · SRB assay

Outside biologyTurning raw spreadsheets into automated reports and rankings.

Problem 03 · image analysis

Did the drug stop the cells, or kill them?

The problem
A compound reduces growth. But is it pausing the cell cycle or causing death? Answering that used to need extra assays.
What I built
An analysis that measures the DNA content of every nucleus from a single DAPI stain and reads cycle phases and apoptosis off the histogram.
Try it
Pick a type of drug and raise the dose. Watch where the cells pile up.

Python · nuclear segmentation · intensity profiling

Outside biologyReading a distribution to tell two possible causes apart.

Problem 04 · statistics · omics

Twenty thousand genes, one mechanism

The problem
We know a compound works, but not how. Sequencing treated cells gives a change for every gene, far too much to read by hand.
What I built
A reproducible RNA-seq workflow: differential expression, sensible thresholds, then pathway enrichment to point at a mechanism.
Try it
Move the thresholds. Too loose and everything looks important; too strict and you lose the signal.

R · DESeq2 · Bioconductor · Snakemake

Outside biologyTesting thousands of hypotheses at once without fooling yourself.
